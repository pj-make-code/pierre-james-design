## Context

Men’s personal care is crowded and confusing: thousands of products, label claims that are hard to decode, and almost no guidance for someone with one specific, long-standing concern like dry scalp or dark spots. Hale curates independent, founder-led brands and matches men to products through a clinician-informed assessment.

## My role

As co-founder and Head of Design I own research, product design and the design system end to end. I also ship front-end features in our React codebase alongside engineering.

## Research

I ran Hale’s foundational user research and turned it into five personas and three buyer segments — replenishment, proactive and reactive. A later segmentation pass proposed a core customer, the Frustrated Optimizer, which I’m validating with a survey joined to real purchase data before we commit our go-to-market.

## Key insight

<Callout label="What changed our direction">
  Men don’t want a routine. They want targeted help with one specific concern —
  and they want to know why a product was picked for them.
</Callout>

## Design decisions

<Columns>
  <Column kicker="Trust" title="“Why we ask” on every question">
    Each assessment question pairs with a plain-language explanation and a cited
    source, so the quiz earns trust through transparency instead of asking for
    it.
  </Column>
  <Column kicker="Choice" title="Three picks per step">
    Results show Best Overall, Best Price and Runner-Up for each routine step,
    then hand off to a cart review where people can swap or add products before
    checkout.
  </Column>
</Columns>

Compliance shaped the interface as much as aesthetics: we removed ingredient concentrations, hid drug-classified actives, and softened “seek / avoid” language across every surface where archetype data renders.

<Toggle title="Rejected alternative: archetype as the headline">
  Early results led with a hair archetype — sixteen types built on curl pattern,
  diameter, porosity and scalp sebum. It was memorable but it buried the thing
  people came for. We kept archetypes as a sub-identity for people with similar
  hair or skin and moved the user’s concern to the front.
</Toggle>
